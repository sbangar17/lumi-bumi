How option B is built (recap)

Your cube is solid glass, so nothing goes inside it. The OLED lies screen-up under the cube and you look down through the glass. Everything else (ESP32, buzzer, wiring) hides in a base below, which is where all the cuteness happens.

Centre the lit area. On the OLED board the lit rectangle (about 22 × 11 mm) sits off-centre. Centre that under the cube, not the whole board, and run its long edge parallel to the front of the cube so the picture isn't sideways.
Check your cube's bottom: it must be flat and polished. A frosted or engraved bottom blurs the picture. Test first by holding the OLED against the glass, powered with the hello-world test.

Six cute bases for a solid cube

Every design uses the same base anatomy. Only the outside changes. All the buttons, ears and decorations sit around the cube, never over it, so the view down into the glass stays clear.

☁️ Cloud nest

Easy–medium$

The cube rests on a fluffy white cloud with a sleepy face. The screen glows like a tiny sky inside the glass.

  • How to make it:
    • Sculpt it in air-dry clay or foam over a small box, or 3D-print it in white and soften it with paint.
    • Or cover a plain wooden or cardboard box in white felt or fleece, with felt cloud bumps stitched on.
  • Buttons: A = a yellow sun (Hi / Miss you), B = a pink heart (Love you / Good night).
  • Sound holes: Raindrop-shaped holes on the underside or back.
  • Extra: A warm LED inside a white, thin plastic cloud makes it glow softly. Needs an extra part.

🧸 Bear paws

Medium$$

A teddy-bear body holds the cube like a belly-button gem. The two buttons are the paws, so pressing them is like holding hands.

  • How to make it:
    • Cut the body from a wooden block (rounded with sandpaper) or 3D-print it. Add two raised arms or ears at the sides (round tops, pink inside), set beside the cube so they never cover the view.
    • Paint it honey brown, with a face on the front.
  • Buttons: Paw pads: put a 12 mm button in each paw and cover with a soft silicone or polymer-clay paw cap (A and B pads in different colours).
  • Sound holes: A pattern of small dots or heart holes in the tummy.
  • Extra: Swap the ears for bunny, cat or fox ears to match her favourite animal.

🌷 Flower pot garden

Easiest$

A real mini terracotta pot is the base. The cube is a glass bubble in a little garden, with ladybug buttons crawling up the rim.

  • How to make it:
    • Buy a small pot (about 8–10 cm across and a 5–6 cm rim). Put a round wooden disc or a piece of acrylic inside it, flush with the top, to carry the OLED and the cube.
    • Paint the pot, add felt leaves and a paper or felt flower on a stem on one side.
  • Buttons: Ladybugs: paint two big button caps red with black spots and place them on the front of the pot.
  • Sound holes: The hollow pot is a natural speaker: put the buzzer inside, facing the drainage hole or a bigger side hole.
  • Extra: The drainage hole is the perfect place for the USB-C cable. Almost no tools needed.

🌊 Lighthouse

Medium–hard$$

The cube is the lamp room on top of a striped tower. Messages are the light that reaches the other lighthouse across the sea.

  • How to make it:
    • A tower about 6–8 cm across with a wide flared foot, so the cube can't tip: turn from wood, 3D-print, or build around a round tin or jar.
    • Paint red and white stripes; add a thin railing under the cube.
  • Buttons: Round portholes as buttons: A and B, in blue rings.
  • Sound holes: A ring of small holes below the stripes.
  • Extra: A pair of gift cards: “Lumi lighthouse” and “Bumi lighthouse”, one for each end, for a story.

🌙 Moon and stars night-light

Easy–medium$

A deep-blue base scattered with stars, with a crescent moon on the shoulder. Perfect on a bedside table: the screen glows like a night sky.

  • How to make it:
    • A box about 7 × 7 × 5 cm in wood, printed plastic or covered cardboard, painted midnight blue.
    • Add star stickers or gold paint and a crescent moon cut from card.
  • Buttons: A = a golden star button, B = a silver moon button.
  • Sound holes: Star-shaped cutouts on the sides let sound out and, with an optional LED inside, glow gold in the dark.
  • Extra: Pairs well with the “night mode” (dim the screen and mute the sound at bedtime).

🌈 Rainbow arch

Medium$$

A rainbow arch stands behind the cube, with little white clouds at each end, on a cream wooden base.

  • How to make it:
    • Cut five arches from thin plywood or foam board, each about 1 cm narrower than the last, paint them in rainbow colours and glue them in a stack, or print the arch in colour. Stand it at the back of the base.
    • The base is a cream wood or painted box with the OLED pocket in the top.
  • Buttons: Round buttons in sunny yellow (A) and pink (B) at the front.
  • Sound holes: Cloud-shaped holes in the back.
  • Extra: The arch is just decoration, so it can be paper craft glued to card if you have no tools.

Build recipe (works for every design)

  1. Measure the cube bottom, the OLED board (about 27 × 28 mm) and the lit area, the ESP32, the buzzer and the buttons.
  2. Mock-up: make the base from cardboard. Tape the OLED under the cube, with the cube centred over the lit area. Power it and run a message from the phone page.
  3. Cut or print the top plate with the opening and the lip. Dry-fit the cube.
  4. Fix the OLED face-up on its rim with a drop of glue at the edges only. Keep glue off the screen.
  5. Fit the ESP32 and buzzer with foam tape. Point the USB-C port at its slot.
  6. Wire the buttons (GPIO3 and GPIO4 to GND), and solder or crimp the joints for the final build.
  7. Add the silicone pad, and lower the cube on it. Check the picture from every side and from above.
  8. Decorate last: paint, felt, ears, stickers. Keep the decorations away from the glass.
  9. Run the full test: both buttons, all four quick messages, text, drawing, photo, and the sound.

Cute things the software can do

Since the picture seems to float inside the glass, simple pictures look best. Ideas I can add:

  • Floating heart idle screen: a heart that bobs gently, or a face that blinks.
  • Greets her by name: "Hi [her name]!" when a message arrives or the cube wakes up.
  • New quick pictures: her favourite animal, a unicorn, a flower, a birthday cake. They need only 8 × 8 pixels each.
  • Lullaby for Good night and happy tunes for Hi and Love you.
  • Night mode: dim and mute between set hours.
  • Brightness setting to suit the thickness of the cube.
Glow upgrade: a small LED or a ring of LEDs (like a NeoPixel ring) under a translucent base can pulse softly when a message comes in. It needs one extra part and some firmware, and I can add it.

Keep it safe and sturdy

  • A solid glass cube is heavy and hard. Use a wide, stable base so it cannot tip off a table. Add felt feet.
  • For a young child, an acrylic cube is much safer than glass. The optics are almost the same.
  • Stick ears, caps and stickers on firmly. No small loose parts.
  • Don't glue the cube down permanently. Use the removable silicone pad so you can service the electronics.
  • Keep the cable short, tucked away and out of reach. Use a proper USB-C charger.
